AI prompts

Make AI your research assistant

Four prompts that plan your stack for your major. Copy one, paste it into the AI you use, and check what comes back. First, three rules:

  1. Never paste your SSN, student ID, address or a password into an AI.
  2. Get official links, and check every deadline on the official site. AI can be out of date.
  3. Your resume is yours. Everything on it must be your own work, and true.

Make the prompts yours

The prompts below fill in as you type. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere: it stays on this page. Without JavaScript, copy a prompt by hand and replace the words in brackets.

1 A skill for this semester

I’m a freshman [your major] student at  in the U.S., with about 5 hours a week outside class.

Which skill should I build this semester that employers in my field look for in interns and new hires?

1. Give me 3–5 options, ranked. For each: why it matters in my field, how long it takes to get useful, and an official or well-known source I can check, with a link.
2. Then take the #1 option (or the one I pick) and give me a week-by-week plan for this semester that fits my hours, with free places to learn and practice.

Never make up deadlines, prices or links. If you aren’t sure, say so and tell me where to check.

You get: a ranked list of skills with sources, then a weekly plan for one of them.

2 Your first certification

I’m a freshman [your major] student at  in the U.S., with about 5 hours a week outside class.

Which certification should I earn first that employers in my field actually ask for?

1. Give me 3–5 options, ranked. For each: who offers it (official link), what it proves to employers, the usual study time, and whether students get a discount. If my field uses licenses or other credentials instead, say so and suggest the best first step.
2. Then take the #1 option (or the one I pick) and give me a week-by-week study plan that fits my hours, with free study videos, practice questions and practice labs.

Never make up deadlines, prices or links. Tell me to check the current price and exam details on the official site.

You get: credentials your field asks for, then a study plan with free resources.

3 Your first project

I’m a freshman [your major] student at  in the U.S., with about 5 hours a week outside class.

What first project could I finish this semester that would impress people who work in my field?

1. Give me 3–5 ideas, ranked by how much they would impress a hiring manager and how doable they are for me. For each: what I would make, which skills it proves, and where I would show it (GitHub, a portfolio, a write-up or a poster).
2. Then take the #1 idea (or the one I pick) and give me a week-by-week plan that fits my hours, with free tools, and show me how to describe it in one resume line when it’s done.

Never make up facts or links. If you aren’t sure, say so.

You get: project ideas ranked for your field, then a plan and your future resume line.

4 Ten programs and internships

I’m a freshman [your major] student at  in the U.S.

Find 10 internships, research programs or early-career programs I could apply to for next summer. Include programs open to first- and second-year students.

1. Put them in a table I can paste into a spreadsheet, with these columns: Program | Organization | Who can apply (year, major, citizenship, GPA) | Deadline or usual window | Paid? | Official link
2. Rank them by how well they fit me, with one line on why.
3. Then take the best fit and give me a week-by-week plan to get the application in before its deadline: resume, essays and recommendation letters.

Only list programs you are confident exist, and link only to official sites. Never guess a deadline: if you aren’t sure, write “check site”.

You get: a table to paste into a spreadsheet. That’s the start of your tracker. Then check every deadline on the program’s own site.

What to do with the answers

  • Check before you trust. Open every link. If a program or deadline isn’t on the official site, cross it out.
  • Keep a tracker. Paste prompt 4’s table into a spreadsheet and add a column for “applied”.
  • Pick one of each. One skill, one certification, one project. Write them on your Stack Card.
  • Come back weekly. Tell the AI how the week went and ask it to adjust the plan.
