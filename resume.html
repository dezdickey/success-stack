Window 1: Your resume

Your first resume, step by step

A recruiter’s first look at a resume lasts about 7.4 seconds on average. Make the top half easy to read, put your best blocks first, and make every line true.

  1. Start with a plain, one-page layout

    One column, standard section headings, a readable font, and no photo, tables or text boxes. Any word processor works. Keep the file you edit, and send a PDF.

  2. Put your contact details at the top

    Your name in the largest text. Under it: your city and state, a professional email, your phone number, and links to your LinkedIn and your GitHub or portfolio.

    Leave off your street address, birthday, Social Security number and student ID.

  3. Education comes first while you’re in school

    Your school, your degree and major, and your expected graduation month and year. Add your GPA if it helps you, plus honors and 3–6 courses that matter for the job.

  4. Skills you could explain in an interview

    Group them: tools, software, lab or clinical skills, languages. If you couldn’t talk about it for a minute, leave it off.

  5. Projects: your strongest section right now

    For each one: a name, one line on what it is, a link if it’s online, and two or three bullets on what you did and what came of it.

  6. Experience: every job counts

    Retail, food service, campus jobs and volunteering show you’re reliable and good with people. Write them with the same bullet formula as everything else.

  7. Certifications, earned or in progress

    “In progress, expected May 2027” is honest, and it shows a plan.

  8. Activities and leadership

    Clubs, roles, volunteering, honors. One line each, with your role and the dates.

  9. Write bullets that prove it

    Action verb + what you did + how (tools) + result (a number if you have one)

    Weak: Responsible for the club’s social media. Stronger: Grew the club’s Instagram from 80 to 240 followers in one semester by posting weekly event recaps.

    That one is a made-up example. Use your own real numbers, and only ones you can back up.

  10. Check it before you send it

    • One page, with dates in one format.
    • No typos: read it out loud, slowly.
    • Saved as a PDF named First_Last_Resume.pdf.

  11. Get feedback, then update it every semester

    Your career center, a professor or an upperclassman can review it. Add each new block as soon as it’s real.

  12. Use AI to polish, never to invent

    First remove your phone number, email and address. Then paste your resume after this prompt:

    Resume review prompt

    Review my resume for an internship in my field. Point out weak or vague bullets and suggest stronger action verbs. Ask me questions to find results I left out. Don’t add anything I didn’t do, and don’t change any facts or numbers.
