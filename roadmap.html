The roadmap

When and where to build your stack

One block a semester adds up fast. Here is when to build each one, when employers hire, and where to look.

When

Add a block every semester

  1. Year 1

    • Fall: one skill, one club, LinkedIn, a first resume.
    • Spring: a first project. Apply to programs for first-years.
    • Summer: a program, research, a job, or a bigger project.

  2. Year 2

    • Fall: apply for summer internships in Aug–Oct. A first certification.
    • Spring: a second project. A role in your club.
    • Summer: your first internship or research program.

  3. Year 3

    • Fall: apply early for the internship you want most.
    • Spring: a project with real users.
    • Summer: the internship that can turn into a job offer.

  4. Year 4

    • Fall: full-time applications. Apply early.
    • Spring: finish strong. Help a first-year build their stack.

Behind? Start with the next block. Every semester counts, and so does every summer.

The recruiting year starts in August

  1. Aug–Oct

    Big employers open next summer’s internships. Many review applications as they arrive, so early beats perfect.

  2. Nov–Jan

    Many government, defense and research-lab programs close.

  3. Feb–Apr

    A second wave: more postings, smaller employers, summer research programs.

  4. May–Jul

    Do the work. Update your resume and LinkedIn. Get ready for August.

This is a typical year, not a rule. Every program sets its own dates, so check each one on its official site.

Where

Where to look

  • Your career center, and Handshake if your school uses it.
  • Your professors. Ask in person about research in their lab.
  • LinkedIn Jobs: search your field, then filter for internships.
  • USAJOBS for students: federal internships, in many majors.
  • NSF REU search: summer research programs for undergraduates.
  • Company career pages: look for “students”, “university” or “early careers”.

Examples from one field, cybersecurity: NREIP, CyberCorps: Scholarship for Service and DoD SMART. To find ten for your major, use prompt 4.
