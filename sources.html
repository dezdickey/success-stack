Sources and method

Where every number comes from

Each number on this site was checked against its source on 4 November 2026. Here is the exact figure, what it means, and its limits.

  1. Burning Glass Institute and Strada Institute for the Future of Work, Talent Disrupted: College Graduates, Underemployment, and the Way Forward, February 2024

    Report page · The full report (PDF)

    • 52%: “52 percent of graduates are underemployed a year after graduation” (p. 6 of the PDF).
    • 73%: of graduates who start out underemployed, 73 percent are still underemployed ten years after college (p. 6).
    • 48.5%: the odds of underemployment are 48.5 percent lower for graduates with at least one internship than for those with none, after controlling for factors such as gender, race and ethnicity, and the kind of college attended (p. 7).
    • What “underemployed” means here: working in a job that doesn’t need a bachelor’s degree.
    • Limits: the data covers U.S. graduates with a bachelor’s degree. The internship figure shows a strong link, not proof that the internship alone caused the better outcome.

  2. National Association of Colleges and Employers (NACE), “Internship Experience Often the Deciding Factor Between Equal Candidates”, 9 February 2026

    The article

    • #1, 4.5 and 4.3: asked what decides between two equally qualified candidates, employers rated “completed internship with your organization” 4.5 and “has internship experience in your industry” 4.3, the top two. Major was rated 3.8, and a high GPA (3.0 or higher) 2.9.
    • The scale: 1 = no influence at all, 5 = extreme influence. The ratings are in the article’s Figure 1.
    • Limits: it comes from NACE’s Job Outlook 2026 survey, answered by 183 employers, most of them NACE members.

  3. NACE, “Intern Conversion Rate Hits Highest Mark in Five Years”, 3 April 2026

    The article

    • 63.1%: the average conversion rate of interns to full-time hires, for 2024–25 interns, from NACE’s 2026 Internship & Co-op Survey.
    • Limits: it averages the employers who answered the survey. Your chances depend on the employer and the field.

  4. HR Dive, “Eye tracking study shows recruiters look at resumes for 7 seconds”, 8 November 2018

    The article

    • 7.4 seconds: “Today’s recruiters skim resumes for an average of 7.4 seconds,” from Ladders’ 2018 eye-tracking study.
    • Limits: this is the first screen of a resume, not the whole review. A resume that passes it gets read more closely.

How the guide was made

  • The Stack (skills, projects, certifications and internships, seen through four windows) is Desmond Dickey’s framework. It is a way to plan, not a finding from the sources above.
  • The roadmap is advice, not data: a typical year. Programs set their own dates.
  • The AI prompts ask for official sources and tell the AI never to guess a deadline. AI can still be wrong, so check everything it says on the official site.
  • Privacy: the site has no cookies, analytics or trackers, and loads nothing from other sites. What you type into the prompt form stays on the page.
